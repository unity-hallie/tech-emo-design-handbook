# bibliography-pending.md — for Hallie to read / vet

**This file holds citations Claude has surfaced but Hallie has
not yet vetted.** It is a deliberate companion to
`bibliography.md` (which contains only vetted, can-stand-behind
citations).

The split exists because **human validation of AI finds is part
of the handbook's process discipline.** The handbook's
credibility depends on every load-bearing citation being one
Hallie has actually read and can defend. AI bibliography-
generation is confident-sounding by default; that confidence is
not the same as warrant. Anything Claude surfaces lives here
until Hallie reads it and either:

- **promotes** it to `bibliography.md` with the `[vetted]` tag,
- **demotes** it to the *do-not-deploy* section below with a
  short note on why, or
- **leaves it here** as worth-reading-eventually.

This process is also a hedge against my (Claude's) failure
modes — confidently citing things that turn out to be
mis-remembered, misattributed, or hallucinated, and confidently
recommending things that turn out to be controversial or
contested in their field in ways I didn't surface.

### Self-Determination Theory (for chapter on agency + self-expression)

Hallie has signaled SDT as load-bearing for the chapter on
*Who You Are When You Write* — specifically autonomy as one
of the three psychological needs (alongside competence and
relatedness) that drive intrinsic motivation. *Reams* of
support in K–12 and higher-ed pedagogy literature, and a
load-bearing claim for the handbook's *agency-to-feather-
the-boundary* engineering case.

- **Deci, Edward L.** *Intrinsic Motivation* (1971/1975).
  Plenum. The foundational text — autonomy, competence,
  intrinsic motivation as distinct from extrinsic reward.
- **Deci, Edward L. and Richard M. Ryan.** *Intrinsic
  Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior*
  (1985). Plenum. The full SDT framework.
- **Ryan, Richard M. and Edward L. Deci.** "Self-Determination
  Theory and the Facilitation of Intrinsic Motivation, Social
  Development, and Well-Being." *American Psychologist* 55:1
  (2000). The standard short-form citation.
- **Ryan, Richard M. and Edward L. Deci.** *Self-Determination
  Theory: Basic Psychological Needs in Motivation, Development,
  and Wellness* (2017). Guilford. Comprehensive recent
  treatment. Probably the right "one book" if Hallie wants
  to vet a single source.

*Cited in the handbook for the structural claim that learning
environments without authentic choice produce measurably
worse engagement and retention. Pair with the trauma-informed
literature's "voice and choice" principle for institutional
applications.*

### Self-Referent Encoding (for chapter on agency + self-expression)

Hallie has signaled self-referent encoding as load-bearing —
specifically the finding that *information encoded with
reference to the self is remembered better than information
encoded otherwise*. The most engineering-shaped of the
psychological literature supporting performance-of-self as a
design variable. Pure memory research; no identity-politics
exposure surface.

- **Rogers, T. B., Kuiper, N. A., and Kirker, W. S.**
  "Self-Reference and the Encoding of Personal Information."
  *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 35:9 (1977).
  *The* citation. Demonstrated that words processed for
  self-relevance are recalled significantly better than the
  same words processed for semantic, phonemic, or structural
  features.
- **Symons, Cynthia S. and Blair T. Johnson.** "The
  Self-Reference Effect in Memory: A Meta-Analysis."
  *Psychological Bulletin* 121:3 (1997). The meta-analytic
  confirmation; ~129 studies pooled. Useful if challenged.
- **Klein, Stanley B. and Judith Loftus.** "The Nature of
  Self-Referent Encoding: The Contributions of Elaborative
  and Organizational Processes." *Journal of Personality and
  Social Psychology* 55 (1988). Refines the mechanism —
  self-reference encoding works via elaboration and
  organization, both. Useful for the engineering-shaped
  framing.

*Cited in the handbook for the claim that letting a learner
bring themselves into the content is not decoration; it is
a memory and retention move with measurable effects.*

### Bartle (for player-type and irreducible-engagement-plurality)

Hallie has signaled Bartle as load-bearing for the
*engagement is irreducibly plural* claim — design that
serves only one type of player loses the others. Game
design has been the testing ground for this claim for
decades; the handbook borrows it for instruction-writing
and learning design.

*Provisional listing — Hallie may already know this work
directly given the Final Form Games background. Confirm
on vetting pass.*

- **Bartle, Richard.** "Hearts, Clubs, Diamonds, Spades:
  Players Who Suit MUDs." *Journal of MUD Research* 1:1
  (1996). The original taxonomy: Achievers, Explorers,
  Socializers, Killers.
- **Bartle, Richard.** *Designing Virtual Worlds* (2003).
  New Riders. The full theoretical treatment of his player
  model and its design implications.
- **Yee, Nick.** "Motivations for Play in Online Games."
  *CyberPsychology & Behavior* 9:6 (2006). Empirical
  refinement of Bartle's taxonomy via factor analysis on
  large MMO populations. Useful citation for the
  *empirically validated* version of the multi-type claim.

### Avatar customization / Proteus Effect (for personalization-as-engagement)

For the empirical case that *the act of letting users
personalize affects subsequent behavior in the environment*.
The Proteus Effect is real; cite carefully because the
popular reading of it sometimes overreaches.

- **Yee, Nick and Jeremy Bailenson.** "The Proteus Effect:
  The Effect of Transformed Self-Representation on Behavior."
  *Human Communication Research* 33:3 (2007). The foundational
  paper. *Confirm replication status on vet — there have been
  challenges to some specific effect sizes; the core finding
  appears robust but the literature is uneven.*
- **Turkay, Selen and Sonam Adinolf.** "The Effects of
  Customization on Motivation in an MMORPG." *Journal of
  Gaming and Virtual Worlds* (2014, approximate). Direct
  evidence that customization → motivation/identification.
  *Confirm specific citation details on vet.*
- **Birk, Max V., Cheralyn Atkins, Jason T. Bowey, and
  Regan L. Mandryk.** "Fostering Intrinsic Motivation
  through Avatar Identification in Digital Games." *CHI*
  (2016). Connects avatar customization directly to SDT's
  intrinsic motivation construct. Useful bridge citation.
