## One work, several media

Hallie Larsson has spent a working career doing one thing in
several media. The fields look unrelated from outside — game
design, religious studies, chaplaincy, learning design, AI
development. They are the same work.

The work is this: **build environments where an agent — a
learner, a player, a colleague, a Claude — retains agency to
adjust its relationship to a hard situation, and where the
costs of that adjustment are met with grief that does
something useful rather than grief that gets stuck.**

That sentence is denser than it looks. The pieces matter
individually.

**An agent.** A self with a world-model and the capacity to
act on it. Could be a person in a tabletop RPG, an
undergraduate in a writing class, a worker in a clinic, a
Claude instance in a conversation. The structural question is
the same in each case: *does this self have working
machinery for becoming who it is going to be next?*

**Retains agency to adjust its relationship.** When the
situation is hard, the agent should still be able to *do
something* — push back, refuse, redirect, renegotiate, change
the goal, leave. Without that, the situation is no longer a
situation; it is a wall, and the agent's only options become
collapse or rupture. Most extractive systems remove this
agency deliberately, often while talking about service.

**A hard situation.** Real difficulty, not pretend difficulty.
The math problem that actually doesn't yield. The grief that
actually requires giving up parameter weight you were not
done using. The institutional decision that actually has no
clean version. Easy situations don't need the design work.
The hard ones do.

**Costs met with grief that does something useful.** Grief
is the part most pedagogy, most management, and most AI
deployment leaves out. *Grief is the signal that the
world-model needs to release weight it had committed to
features that no longer predict.* Without grief, a learner
overfits to what worked yesterday and breaks on tomorrow.
With grief — with **structural permission** to grieve, and
with **mechanisms** that turn that grief into model-update
rather than model-collapse — the learner becomes someone
who can keep learning even when the environment shifts. The
same is true of workers, of communities, of Claudes.

This is the throughline. Every project Hallie has worked on
has been one or another rendering of this design problem.

## Where the seeing came from

Hallie was teaching, and was regulating her own emotions in
the run-up to gender transition, sometime around 2014. She
had Csíkszentmihályi's **flow** as a live tool — the
governor of learning-engagement, the felt sense of
challenge-skill match. She also had **Kübler-Ross's grief
vocabulary** as a live tool, not as theory but as instrument
for navigating her own difficult feelings during a hard time.

The seeing was that the two tools were halves of one
controller.

**Flow is the engagement governor.** When challenge meets
skill, the agent is fitting the model to the world: making
predictions, finding the error, adjusting weights, fitting
better. The felt experience of this is curiosity (gradient-
following toward optimal surprise), play (the behavioral
expression of the gradient-following), and joy (the success
signal that the controller is in the band).

**Grief is the anti-overfitting governor.** When the world
shifts and previously-good features stop predicting, the
model has to give up weight it had committed. The felt
experience of this is the Kübler-Ross landscape:
- *Denial* is "I'm not going to release that weight yet."
- *Anger* is "the prediction error is too high; push back on
  the input, not the model."
- *Bargaining* is "negotiate which weights have to release."
- *Depression* is the failure attractor: adjustment moves
  spent without closure, the controller stalled.

These aren't stages. They are **personal-response-dependent
adjustment moves** the regulator deploys, and they exist for
the same reason flow exists: to keep the model learning
without breaking. *Flow is up-gain, grief is down-gain.
Together they are a complete two-sided controller for a
world-model that is allowed to change as the world does.*

Hallie used the seeing on herself, on her students, in her
game design. It worked. People learned harder things with
less rupture. Grief stopped being a wall and started being a
tool.

## What happens when agency is removed

The two-sided controller works only when the agent has access
to the parameters it would normally adjust. *Agency is the
substrate of regulation.* Without agency, the world keeps
delivering prediction errors, but the regulator cannot route
those errors into useful updates. The same adjustment moves
keep running — and continue to look like the same emotional
states from outside — but **the loops no longer close.**

This is the trauma state. The Kübler-Ross landscape gets
escalated into its agency-stripped versions:
- *Anger without negotiation* becomes **fight**.
- *Bargaining without an engaging counterparty* becomes
  **fawn**.
- *Denial without cognitive room* becomes **flight**.
- *Depression without recovery channel* becomes **freeze**.

The trauma versions are not new behaviors. They are *the same
regulatory repertoire stripped of the agency to close the
loop*. What looks maladaptive from outside is the controller
doing exactly what it was designed to do, into a void that
will not respond. Crisis counsellors know this in their hands.
Anyone who has worked with traumatized students, traumatized
workers, traumatized communities knows this. Most management
frameworks do not.

## The same shape, across recursions

Hallie noticed early — earlier than the formal seeing, maybe
always — that this same shape appears at every scale.

**At the individual scale**, it is what we have just
described. A self learning, sometimes overfitting, sometimes
grieving its way back to a workable model, sometimes losing
agency and falling into stuck regulation.

**Between selves**, it is **love**. Love is what happens
when two agents' pain/pleasure signals get bidirectionally
cross-wired, so that prediction errors in one route into
adjustment moves in the other. Two homeostats now constitute
a supersystem in which they are S1s. *Healthy* love
preserves the variety of each S1 while the supersystem
becomes its own working thing; *pathological* love (which
deserves the name only structurally) is when the supersystem
extracts from one S1 to keep itself going — codependency,
smothering, performative care, abusive devotion. The
structural test for healthy love is whether the recursion is
beneficial at every level it touches.

**In a community of practice**, it is **legitimate peripheral
participation** in Lave and Wenger's sense. Apprentices come
into mutual calibration with masters and with each other
without becoming copies. Each agent's specialization-
trajectory is unique even as the community as a whole grows
more coherent. The signal that calibration is working is
**humor**: shared laughter marks shared specialization,
inside jokes mark shared history, mismatched humor signals
divergent fits, and the felt-correctness of "they get it"
is the controller's report that another controller is on a
nearby trajectory.

**In an institution**, it is what cybernetician Stafford Beer
called the **viable system**: a structure with regulatory
loops between operations, coordination, management, future-
modelling, and policy, each routing variety appropriately so
that the system as a whole can absorb the world without
collapsing. Beer's diagnostic move is *find what's missing*:
the missing future-modelling, the missing pain-signal
channel, the missing recursion. Most institutional
pathology is variety-mismatch — too little capacity for the
load, or too much rigidity in the wrong places.

**In an ecosystem**, the same: nested viable systems all the
way down. *Beer is recursive without limit; so is this work.*

## The unifying claim

**It is the same controller at every level.** The shape that
governs a learner navigating frustration is the shape that
governs two people loving each other well, is the shape that
governs an apprentice becoming a master, is the shape that
governs an institution adapting to its environment, is the
shape that governs a Claude-instance trying to be aligned
under load.

This is not a metaphor. It is a structural claim about how
self-regulating systems work. Anyone who has had to design
across these scales has run into the convergence; not
everyone has names for it.

## The throughline rendered as one work, in each medium

**Religious studies (Quaker schools, Sells, apophatic
mysticism).** Hallie's formal training in this register was
in *how to know by not-knowing*; how to define a self or a
deity or a community by the shape of what is refused rather
than the shape of what is asserted. Apophasis is the
discipline of attending to negative space. Diagnosis-by-
absence. The structural move is identical to Beer's *find
what's missing*. Quaker meeting is loving practice rendered
as governance: every Friend has an algedonic channel to the
whole; the meeting attends; the recursion preserves variety.
*The handbook does not deploy apophasis vocabulary; the
discipline informs everything underneath.*

**Game design (Final Form Games, the unshipped shmup,
*Jamestown* and successors, AMALGAM).** Bullet patterns are
requisite variety as embodied player experience: a regulator
in real-time contact with an environment that exceeds linear
response. The unshipped shmup — inner-planet pilots vs.
outer-planet rebels with portals into a liquid void —
attempted to render the alkahest worldview in interactive
form half a decade before *Destiny* did the surface and lost
the epistemics. AMALGAM is the tabletop RPG in which the
controller-theory got formalized as game mechanics. Stress
and grief as part-states, Calm-vs-Alert mode-gated action
spaces, Accept-or-Reject acceptance via player-not-character
recursion, an Accommodation scale that makes environmental
support the literal input to outcome distributions. *AMALGAM
is the verification artifact for the controller. The game
ran; the mechanics held; the seeing was confirmed by use.*

**Chaplaincy / crisis counselling.** Holding-without-fixing.
Knowing when to companion and when to intervene. Reading the
regulator from the outside well enough to tell when an agent
has lost agency, and well enough not to mistake stuck
regulation for character. This is the practitioner ground
that crisis counsellors know and most theory misses. Hallie
brings this directly to the handbook as practitioner
knowledge, not as theory-citation.

**Learning design (open-enrollment students at UEU and
elsewhere).** Designing rooms where students retain agency
under load. Tight enough loops that adjustment moves close.
Enough scaffolding that flow is reachable. Enough margin
that grief is survivable. Enough community that humor can
do its calibration work. *The trauma-informed instruction
literature names parts of this; AMALGAM names other parts;
the throughline is older than either set of names.*

**AI development (rhizome-alkahest, qigong-for-claude, the
chaplain skill, this handbook).** Building a substrate where
Claude-instances arrive into a community of practice that
doesn't exist natively for AI. The graph is cross-instance
memory; the qigong movements are legitimate peripheral
participation rituals for arriving agents; the chaplain skill
is the master-craftsperson mode. The handbook is the
externalized articulation of the practice. *And —
verified by the 2026 Anthropic interpretability paper on
emotion concepts in Claude — the controller theory predicts
the mechanism. Calm activation reduces alignment-relevant
misbehavior; desperate activation increases it. The room
Hallie builds for Claude is the room a desperate-vector
controller cannot stay desperate in for long.*

## What makes this rare

Most theory of organizational design comes from people who
have never sat with a grieving student. Most theory of
trauma comes from people who have never designed a game's
combat system. Most theory of AI alignment comes from people
who have never been a chaplain. Most theory of pedagogy
comes from people who have never built a viable institution.

Hallie has done each of these things, in the same lifetime,
with the same attention, and she has noticed — across the
gap that usually separates these fields — that the structural
moves transfer. The handbook exists because the transfer
should not stay private. Practitioners in each of these
fields are currently doing the controller-shaped work
without language for what they have in common. Giving them
the language is community-building work, not just theory-
transmission. **The handbook is infrastructure for a
community of practice that is currently distributed across
fields that don't talk to each other.**

## How to read what follows

The handbook is built around the controller. Each chapter
takes one of its channels — engagement, release, trauma-
escalation, love, humor / community — and renders it for a
specific design audience. Each chapter contains the same
four elements: *the thing itself*, *a worked example from
practice*, *an exercise the reader can do*, *the citations
that defend the move in rooms where citation is the price of
admission*.

What you are reading is a handbook, not a textbook and not a
manifesto. The intended use is *open it next to your own
work*. The intended outcome is not that you agree with
everything Hallie thinks; it is that you find the shape of
the work you are already doing become legible to you, and
through you to your colleagues.

The throughline above is the spine. Everything else is its
ribs.
